Greenhouse Gas Emissions: White Hat vs. Black Hat visualizations

Black Hat Visualization Manipulative Techniques

  1. Data Transformation: Pollution data were transformed into values relative to 2010 pollution levels, which only allows for one perspective of the data as absolute levels of pollution are completely obscured by this transformation.
  2. Titles/Lables: The unit of measure is denoted in the original dataset only as "index." In our visualization we refer to it as "Pollution Index," which is still an perfectly true description of the unit of measure. By not specifying how this index is calculated, audiences may infer it is a much more involved and nuanced computation than it actually is and come to the conclusion that "since Türkiye has such a higher pollution index than Japan and Germany, it must be a very dirty country and has a larger responsibility to the global community to do its part in resolving the climate change problem."
  3. Axes: The data is truncated to begin at 2012, excluding the year 2010 where "index" values for all countries are 100. This prevents audiences from realizing how the index is actually calculated, allowing for the deception above to work.
  4. Axes: The y-axis is truncated to begin at 65 in order to magnify the disparity in "Pollution Index" between countries. This is to cause audiences to confer more scrutiny upon countries like Türkiye for their perceived greater contribution to the climate problem.

White Hat Visualization Design Choices

The dataset consists of pollution data of each country from 1990 to 2024. Given that the data is a time series, a line graph was chosen to communicate the trends over time. To contrast the deception of the black hat visualization, both absolute and the transformed relative values for pollution were plotted in order to communicate the full picture of pollution trends. Color was chosen to encode country as a more effective channel than alternate choices such as line type. In contrast to the black hat visualization, the plot of the "index" measurement starts at 2010, and the axis title is descriptive of what the index actually means; these allow the true meaning of the values in the plot to be accurately conveyed.

Given the exigence of the climate problem and the need for an understanding of the problem for future planning, a forecast was incorporated into the visualization. Forecasts were also given uncertainty intervals, slightly lighter saturation, and added to the legend for clarity to distinguish forecast values from actual measured values.

Extra Credit Interaction (see at the bottom of the webpage)

The dataset consists of pollution data of each country from 1990 to 2024 as well as seen in the white hat and black hat visualizations. Given the severity and insights of the Greenhouse data, we wanted to highlight and quiz the user on what the potential risks are of having a black hat versus a white hat visualization. It is made clear that just one small change of omitting some years of data can completely alter how we view the trends of Greenhouse emissions. After already viewing our original black hat and white hat visualizations, the user has the option to now filter between a good and bad visualization by clicking a button called "change my hat" effectively switching the visualization from a black hat to a white hat and vice versa. In real time you can see the data trends being transformed just by adding and removing some years from the x-axis. The goal is that after viewing our webpage the user will have a clear understanding of the importance of any visualization, as they can sometimes determine very important actions we need to make.
